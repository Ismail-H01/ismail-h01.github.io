About

MACCS+: Matter-enabled curtain and lighting controller

Final year project, BEng (Hons) Electrical and Electronic Engineering

The problem: curtains are a daily task that is hard with limited mobility, and many smart-home products only work inside one vendor's ecosystem. The answer: a self-designed controller for a curtain and LED lighting that joins a smart home through Matter, and that still works from physical buttons when the network is down.

  • Custom power PCB with reverse polarity protection, hardware soft start, polyfuses, a TVS diode and a 12 V to 5 V buck stage. The 5 V rail held 5.02 V under full load.
  • Firmware in C and C++ on an ESP32-C6 using ESP-IDF and FreeRTOS, with limit switch interrupts, a stall timeout and position saved across power cycles.
  • Matter control commissioned into Apple Home over Bluetooth LE, with Siri voice commands. Five control paths (Matter, web page, buttons, scheduler, REST API) run at once without conflict.
  • Mechanical design in Fusion 360: a belt-driven, centre-opening mechanism with 3D printed parts, including a sacrificial part that protects the assembly on overrun.
  • Debugging: a limit switch feedback loop in the Matter delegate was traced to a command being written from a status callback, and fixed.
  • 92tests: 83 passed, 7 partial, 2 failed
  • 5.02 V5 V rail under full load
  • 30 minsoak test, no crash or hang

Reported openly: Alexa did not discover the device and effect switching through Matter did not work. Next: reposition the limit switches to remove overtravel, find the Alexa fault and add over-the-air updates.

02Autumn 2025

Three-phase AC-DC-AC converter and isolated gate drive

EE3PEC power electronics paper, Aston University

The problem: a converter model is only convincing if hardware agrees with it. The answer: a MATLAB/Simulink model of a rectifier, DC link and three-phase inverter, paired with a gate drive circuit designed, built and tested on the bench, with measured waveforms compared against the simulation.

  • Model: single-phase then three-phase rectifier feeding a voltage-source inverter and a 20 kW balanced load, at 50 Hz fundamental, modulation index 0.8, 750 Hz switching and 10 µs dead-time.
  • Two design additions to the converter: limiting capacitor inrush stress and limiting DC-link overvoltage, in line with industrial practice.
  • Gate drive PCB with galvanic isolation between controller logic and the power stage, and matched tracks on both channels so signal delay stays equal.
  • Control: PWM with dead-time generated on a Microsemi SmartFusion board, programmed with Libero and SoftConsole.
  • Debugging: a transistor pin-out mismatch between the datasheet and the schematic library was caught during soldering, before power-up.
  • 30 Vgate drive swing, +15 V to −15 V
  • ~8 µsdead-time measured on the oscilloscope
  • ~10 nsoutput rise and fall time

Reported openly: experimental waveforms matched the simulation in structure, with some distortion and instances of shoot-through, likely caused by three separate gate drives with different propagation delays (about 300 ns to 1 µs). Next: optimise the dead-time.

Electronics placement at Renishaw

Industrial placement, Encoder Product Division

A year inside a professional electronics design team, working on PCB design, test planning and qualification testing for precision encoder products. This summary stays at a general level.

  • Test jig PCB redesigned from scratch in Zuken CR-8000, working from legacy documentation. Obsolete parts were replaced and the board was made to fit the existing jig. It reached the board testing stage before the placement ended.
  • ESD qualification: a self-directed contact discharge sweep up to ±12 kV on a replacement protection device, with a risk assessment completed first. I presented the findings to senior engineers, and the report was approved for production.
  • Protection investigation: structured pulse testing, including at elevated temperature, to assess whether a protection component was needed. The results were reviewed and signed off by stakeholders.
  • Other work: a PCB for a project at another company site, test plan writing, cable builds, and professional soldering training down to 0402 parts.
  • Outreach: engineering demonstrations and a careers talk as a STEM Ambassador, and a second-place finish in the Early Careers Network Challenge (see Leadership).

How I work

Habits that show up in the projects.

  • 01

    Check against the source

    Hand measurements suggested 18 mm hole spacing on the motor bracket. The datasheet said 17 mm. Dimensions, pin-outs and ratings get checked against primary documents before anything is printed or soldered, which also caught a transistor pin-out mismatch on the gate drive board.

    See MACCS+
  • 02

    Test it, then report what happened

    The final year project was validated against 92 tests. The results list seven partial passes and two failures with causes where known. A result that cannot yet be explained is still a result worth recording.

    See the results
  • 03

    Keep layers independent

    The motor driver knows nothing about Matter, and the limit switches stop the motor whatever the software believes. Separating hardware drivers from protocol code made faults far easier to isolate.

    See the design
  • 04

    Learn the tool, keep the fundamentals

    Proteus fundamentals carried over to Altium and Zuken CR-8000. CAD was new for the mechanical design, and ESD testing was new on placement, so each was learned in small steps before the real work started.

    See the placement
